    How it works

    Three steps, all on your own computer.

    1. Find and identify

      The fish is outlined and compared against 165 species known from California waters. Lookalikes with different rules are flagged.

    2. Measure

      Length follows the fish's midline from snout to tail, curved or not, in total or fork length as CDFW measures that species. Pixels become inches using an object of known size.

    3. Apply the rules

      Species, length, place, date and fishing mode are checked against the current regulations. Every rule shown links to its section of Title 14.

    Getting an exact length

    In the photoAccuracy
    Dollar bill or credit card, flat beside the fishabout 2–3%
    Saved gear, or two points you mark on a known lengthabout 3–5%
    Nothing of known sizeNot measured. One photo can't distinguish a small fish up close from a large fish farther away.

    Verdicts

    Keep
    Legal species, size, season and location, as far as a photo can show.
    Too close
    The measured range overlaps a size limit, or there was no ruler. Measure it on a board.
    Check rules
    Depends on something a photo can't show, such as a hatchery fin clip or a marine protected area's allowed take.
    Release
    Undersized, oversized, protected, out of season, or inside a no-take reserve.

    My gear

    Objects you often photograph with your catch, with their exact length. When one appears next to a fish it's used as the ruler.
