    How it works

    1. 📷

      Photo

      The app finds every fish in the picture and outlines it, along with anything it can use as a ruler.

    2. 📏

      Measure

      It traces the fish's midline from snout to tail — curved fish included — and converts pixels to inches using the best scale it can find.

    3. 📘

      Regs

      The species, length, place and date are checked against the current CDFW rules, and every answer cites the section it came from.

    Tips for a precise length

    • Put a dollar bill or a credit card flat next to the fish, in the same plane. That gets you within about 3%.
    • Shoot from directly above, with the whole fish in frame and its mouth closed.
    • No reference? Save your rod handle or cooler lid under My Gear, or use “Measure with two clicks”.
    • Without any reference the app estimates distance from the photo itself — expect ±10–20%, and it will say “too close to call” near a limit.

    What the verdicts mean

    • Keep Legal species, size, season and place — as far as a photo can tell.
    • Too close The measured range overlaps a size limit. Put it on a ruler.
    • Check rules Depends on something a photo can't show (hatchery fin clip, local closures, MPA rules).
    • Release Undersized, oversized, prohibited, out of season, or inside a no-take reserve.

    My Gear

    Things you often have in photos, with their exact length. When one shows up next to a fish, it's used as a ruler.

      About

      California Fish ID runs on your own computer. Photos never leave it.

      • Detection: SAM 3 or YOLOE finds and outlines the fish and any reference object.
      • Species: BioCLIP 2, limited to fish known from California.
      • Length: midline of the fish outline, scaled by a dollar bill / card / coin / can, your saved gear, two clicks, or Depth Pro as a last resort.
      • Regulations: entered by hand from the 2026 CDFW Ocean and Freshwater Sport Fishing Regulations, with section citations. Verified —.

      Not legal advice — verify at wildlife.ca.gov. Regulations change in season; the app can be wrong about species or size. You are responsible for the fish you keep.
